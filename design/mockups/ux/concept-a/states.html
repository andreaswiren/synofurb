Confirm: erase DSM, keep volumes

Advanced mode. Same gate, different consequences.

⚠
This erases DSM. Your data volume is left alone on a best-effort basis, with no guarantee, and there is no undo.Back up first. A backup you have not tested is a hope, not a backup.

WILL BE DESTROYED

  • DSM itself: the system and swap area on all 4 disks (about 10 GiB each), its settings, users, installed packages, shared-folder definitions and Hyper Backup tasks

WILL BE CHANGED (backed up first)

  • The boot flash: boot-menu entries and files are added (SYNOFURB_BOOT, DEBIAN, DEBIAN_OLD). Synology’s own entry stays, and stays the default until Debian is installed. The whole flash is saved to dom-backups/DS918plus-1930PDN123456-20261101-1402.img (128 MiB, checksum verified) before the first write. Keep that file off the NAS.

LEFT ALONE (best effort, no guarantee)

  • The files on volume1 (btrfs, 10.9 TiB, 6.2 TiB in use): not part of any write command; the installer re-checks their RAID headers at the end
  • Disks in USB / eSATA / expansion units (unplug them anyway)

A failing disk, a power cut or a bug can still destroy them. Without a backup you are betting on it.

For the next 25 minutes

  • Keep this computer awake and on the network. The app prevents sleep; do not close the window.
  • The NAS needs Internet access: Debian packages are downloaded during the install.
  • Do not power off the NAS. Unplug USB and eSATA disks now.
  • Afterwards the data array syncs for about 9 hours. The NAS works meanwhile with reduced protection.

  • Disk sdc reports 8 reallocated sectors (SMART). It is in the data RAID and holds a system copy. A failing disk during the first RAID sync (about 9 hours) is a common way to lose data. Consider replacing it first.

If something goes wrong

  1. Before installer step 3 (cancel is free): DSM is still the default boot entry. If anything fails, the NAS sets DSM as default and reboots after 60 s. Cancelling does the same. Our entries stay on the boot flash until you remove them.
  2. From step 3 on: the first disk is changed and DSM’s own system may be gone. On a failure the installer stops, keeps a rescue shell open for 15 minutes so you can read the log, then makes DSM the default and reboots into the DSM loader. The loader shows Synology’s install page; you reinstall DSM from Synology (find.synology.com). We promise nothing about your data.
  3. Saved on this computer: the boot-flash image above, the plan, the install script and the log. Also the first and last 1 MiB of every disk we change (partition tables, GPT headers). That is metadata only, not your files, and it cannot bring back a formatted filesystem. In Wipe mode it will not help you recover data.

What Keep mode leaves, and what it does not

  • Gone: DSM itself, its settings, users, packages and shared-folder definitions, Hyper Backup tasks.
  • Left alone, best effort: the files on volume1. Not mounted automatically; Debian mounts btrfs/ext4 volumes read-only when you import them in the management plane.
  • Not guaranteed: a failing disk, a power cut during repartitioning or a bug can destroy them. Backups are not optional.
